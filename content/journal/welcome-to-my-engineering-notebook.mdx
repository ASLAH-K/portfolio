# Welcome

If you're reading this, you've probably clicked on the **Learning Journal** section of my website.

Before diving into technical topics, I wanted the first entry to answer a simple question.

**Why does this page exist?**

## Why I Built This

When I first created my portfolio, it served a single purpose.

It showcased my projects, skills, certifications, and resume.

Over time, I realized something was missing.

A portfolio shows **what** you've built.

It rarely shows **how** you think.

It doesn't capture the questions you asked, the mistakes you made, the experiments that failed, or the small moments where something finally clicked.

Those are often the most valuable parts of becoming an engineer.

So instead of treating this website as a static portfolio, I decided to turn it into something that could grow with me.

A **Digital Engineering Workspace**.

---

# What This Journal Is

This isn't a blog.

It isn't built for SEO.

It isn't designed to chase views or publish content on a schedule.

Think of it as my engineering notebook.

Whenever I build something interesting...

Whenever I learn a new concept...

Whenever I spend hours debugging a problem...

Whenever I discover why a system behaves the way it does...

I'll probably write about it here.

Some entries may be detailed.

Some may simply document a question I haven't answered yet.

Both are valuable.

---

# What You'll Find Here

Most of the entries will revolve around topics I'm actively exploring, such as:

- Detection Engineering
- Security Operations (SOC)
- Windows Internals
- Endpoint Monitoring
- Threat Detection
- Digital Forensics
- PowerShell
- Python Automation
- AI in Cybersecurity

But I don't want to limit this journal to a fixed list.

If I become curious about something, and I think it's worth exploring, it belongs here.

---

# Writing to Learn

One thing I've realized is that writing forces me to organize my thoughts.

If I can explain an idea clearly, I probably understand it.

If I struggle to explain it, that's usually a sign that I need to learn more.

So this journal isn't just documentation.

It's part of my learning process.

---

# It's Okay Not to Know Everything

Some entries here will end with complete answers.

Others won't.

Sometimes I'll document an experiment that didn't work.

Sometimes I'll leave with more questions than I started with.

I'm okay with that.

Engineering isn't about always having the right answer.

It's about being curious enough to keep looking for it.

---

# Looking Ahead

This workspace will continue evolving alongside my career.

Projects will grow.

New scripts will be added.

Ideas will change.

Some opinions may even be proven wrong.

Rather than hiding that evolution, I'd like this website to document it.

Because I think growth is far more interesting than perfection.

---

Thanks for stopping by.

Now, let's start building, learning, and figuring things out.
