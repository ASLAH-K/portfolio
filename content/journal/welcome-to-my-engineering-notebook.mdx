# Welcome

Welcome to my Digital Engineering Workspace.

If you're reading this, you've probably clicked on the **Learning Journal** section of my website.

Before writing about cybersecurity, projects, or anything technical, I wanted the very first journal entry to answer one simple question.

**Why does this page exist?**

---

# Why I Built This

Like many students, I started with a portfolio.

It had my projects, skills, certifications, resume, and the usual information recruiters expect to see.

It did its job.

But after a while, I realized it was only showing the final result.

It never showed the questions I asked.

It never showed the mistakes I made.

It never showed the ideas that didn't work, the experiments that failed, or the moments where something finally clicked after hours of debugging.

Those moments are a huge part of learning.

And honestly...

They're often the most interesting part.

So instead of treating this website as a static portfolio, I decided to turn it into something that could grow alongside me.

That's how this **Digital Engineering Workspace** was born.

---

# More Than a Portfolio

Yes, this website is my portfolio.

You'll find my projects here.

You'll find my scripts, certifications, resume, and experience.

But I wanted it to be more than a place that simply says,

> "Here's what I've built."

I wanted a place that also answers questions like:

- Why did I build it?
- What problem was I trying to solve?
- What did I learn?
- What mistakes did I make?
- What would I do differently today?

Those answers usually don't fit on a resume.

They belong here.

---

# What This Journal Is

This isn't a blog.

It isn't built to chase views.

It isn't built around SEO.

I don't have a publishing schedule.

Think of it more like my engineering notebook.

Whenever I build something interesting...

Whenever I learn a concept that finally makes sense...

Whenever I spend hours debugging something...

Whenever I ask a question that sends me down a rabbit hole...

I'll probably write about it here.

Some entries might be detailed.

Some might just capture a small idea before I forget it.

Both are equally valuable.

---

Most of my writing will probably revolve around things I'm currently exploring, including:

That list will change over time.

If I become curious about something new, it'll probably find its way into this workspace.

---

# Writing Helps Me Learn

One thing I've noticed is that writing forces me to slow down.

If I can explain an idea clearly, I probably understand it.

If I struggle to explain it...

I probably have more learning to do.

So these journal entries aren't just documentation.

They're part of how I learn.

Sometimes writing helps me organize ideas that felt messy in my head.

Sometimes it helps me realize that I misunderstood something completely.

I'm okay with both outcomes.

---

# It's Okay Not to Know Everything

I'm still at the beginning of my cybersecurity journey.

I'm learning every day.

Some journal entries will end with clear conclusions.

Others won't.

Sometimes I'll solve the problem.

Sometimes I'll leave with even more questions than I started with.

That's perfectly fine.

I don't want this website to pretend that I always have the answers.

I'd rather document the process honestly than only publish polished success stories.

---

# This Workspace Will Grow With Me

This website isn't finished.

In fact, I hope it never feels finished.

As I learn more, this workspace will grow.

New projects will appear.

Old projects will improve.

Scripts will be added.

Ideas will change.

Some opinions I have today might turn out to be wrong in the future.

Instead of hiding that evolution, I'd like this workspace to document it.

Because I think growth is far more interesting than perfection.

---

# Questions I'm Currently Asking

One thing you'll probably notice is that I ask a lot of questions.

Sometimes they're technical.

Sometimes they're about how the cybersecurity industry works.

Sometimes they're just random thoughts that refuse to leave my head.

Questions like:

- Why are security tools designed this way?
- Why do some detection rules work better than others?
- Could there be a simpler solution?
- Why isn't this idea used more often?

Not every question needs an immediate answer.

Sometimes asking the right question is where learning starts.
